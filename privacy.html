Privacy

Snowstar.Company — Tel Aviv

Last updated 15 October 2026

This page explains what Snowstar.Company collects when you use snowstar.company, the Mutra music catalog and StreamDAW, why, and how to get rid of it. We have deliberately kept the collection small.

Visiting the site

We count page views and track plays so we know what people listen to: a random id held only for the current browser tab, the country your request arrived from, and the site that referred you. If you are signed in, these counts also carry your account; that link is removed after 24 months, or as soon as you delete your account. We do not use advertising cookies, and we honour “Do Not Track” and Global Privacy Control — if either is on, we record nothing at all.

We do not keep IP addresses in readable form. Where we need one to stop abuse (repeated sign-in attempts, contact messages, problem reports, StreamDAW live links), we keep a keyed, shortened code made from it, which cannot be turned back into the address. Records used to slow down repeated sign-in attempts are deleted a day later. Our hosting provider, Cloudflare, sees your IP address in order to deliver the site, as any host does.

Accounts

If you create an account we store your email address, your name if you give one, and your saved favorites. Passwords are never stored — only a salted, stretched hash that cannot be reversed. If you sign in with Google or Facebook we receive your name, email address and profile picture from them, and nothing else; we never receive your password, and we cannot post anything or read your contacts.

Newsletter emails are only sent if you tick the box yourself. Ticking nothing means we never email you except about something you asked for, such as a password reset.

Who we share it with

We do not sell, rent or trade personal data. The third parties involved are the services that run the site: Cloudflare (hosting, database, storage, delivery and receiving our email), GitHub Pages (the pages themselves), Resend (sending our emails, such as receipts and password resets) and, if you buy something, HYP (card payments and tax invoices). They process data on our behalf, not for their own purposes. Our pages load their typefaces from Google Fonts, so your browser asks Google for them and Google sees your IP address and browser. Google or Facebook are involved only if you choose to sign in with them. Our own notifications (a new order, a new account, a problem report) reach us in a Gmail inbox. More on StreamDAW below.

StreamDAW

StreamDAW is our Mac app that streams your DAW to people's browsers. This part covers what it collects on our website, inside the app on your Mac, and from the people who join your streams.

The short version. Your audio, your video and what anyone says in a session never reach us. They go from your Mac to the people you invite, and nowhere else. What reaches us is what any shop needs (your email, your order, the Mac your licence is for), stream-health figures unless you switch them off, usage statistics only if you agree, and problem reports if you write one.

No law requires you to give us any of this. Without an email address we cannot give you an account, sell you Pro or send you a key, and without your Mac's machine ID we cannot make a key. Everything else is optional.

Who is responsible

When you run a stream, the session runs on your Mac. What your listeners send to it (their names, voices and cameras) stays between you and them. We never receive it. If you record someone in your DAW, that recording is yours, and so is the duty to tell them.

Your account, the download and buying Pro

  • Downloading StreamDAW needs a Snowstar account: your email address, your name if you give one, and either a password (stored only as a salted, peppered hash) or a Google or Facebook sign-in. We note which version you downloaded and when.
  • Buying Pro or the 7-day pass: we keep your email, what you bought, the price, any coupon, the order number, and the payment processor's reference for the payment. If you buy from inside the app, the order also carries your Mac's machine ID and the name for your licence.
  • Card details are typed into the page of our payment processor, HYP, and go only to HYP. We never see or store your card number. HYP issues the tax invoice and emails it to you.
  • Your licence key is made for one Mac's machine ID (a code derived from that Mac's hardware, shown in StreamDAW › Settings › Licence). The key carries your licence name, your email and that machine ID. We keep a copy so we can send it again.
  • Emails. We send receipts, a private download link (good for 7 days) and your key. We keep a copy of each one for 2 years, so you can find it again in your account.
  • Newsletter: only if you tick the box when you sign up. It is never ticked in advance. Every newsletter has a one-click unsubscribe. Receipts and keys are not marketing.

What the app sends us

StreamDAW checks your licence on your Mac, offline. It does not phone home to do it.

Stream health, while you are live. Every 30 seconds: connection quality (jitter, packet loss, dropouts, bit rate, frame rate), how many people are connected, and for each of them their type of device (iPhone, Android, Mac…), how long they have been connected, and their connection quality. It is how we keep streams working, so it is on unless you switch it off in StreamDAW › Settings › Usage.

Usage statistics, only if you agree. The first time StreamDAW starts it asks whether to share them, and sends none until you say yes. After each session: how long it ran, how many people and devices joined, which features were used (camera, screen, talkback, in-ear mode…), your app version and whether you are on Free, the trial or Pro. You can change your mind at any time in StreamDAW › Settings › Usage.

  • Neither contains audio, video, chat, names, the session's name, track names or IP addresses, and we do not keep the address they come from.
  • Both are labelled with a code derived from your machine ID. We can link that code to your licence, so we treat them as personal data (pseudonymous), not as anonymous.

Connecting your Mac. At its first start StreamDAW opens a page on this site that connects the Mac to your Snowstar account: we store its machine ID with your account. From then on stream health and, if you allowed it, usage statistics from that Mac are linked to you, so we can see how your trial went and help when something does not work. Deleting your data removes the connection and those rows.

Update checks. At launch and once a day StreamDAW asks us whether a newer version exists: it sends only its version numbers and a random install code made for this alone (not your machine ID); we keep which versions are in use, never the IP address, and forget an install unseen for 180 days.

Your live link. If you use a snowstar.company/live/… link, your Mac tells us the current address of its Cloudflare tunnel each time it changes, so the link keeps working. We store the link's id, one-way codes of your Mac's machine ID and of its secret, the app version, and a keyed code made from the IP address it came from (to stop abuse; it cannot be turned back into the address). A link unused for 180 days is deleted.

Problem reports. If you or a listener press "Report a problem", we receive what was written, an email address only if one is given, and technical context: app or browser version, connection figures, and the session's name. No logs are attached. A keyed code made from the IP address limits how many reports one connection can send. Reports are kept for 2 years.

What stays on your Mac

StreamDAW keeps the following on your Mac. We cannot see any of it, and it reaches us only if you send it to us yourself.

  • The rewind buffer: a recording of your master output during the session, so listeners can go back while you are live. It is deleted when the session ends (and, if StreamDAW stopped unexpectedly, the next time it starts). If your DAW mixes a listener's voice into the master, the voice is in it too.
  • Logs in ~/Library/Logs/StreamDAW, for fixing problems. They include the names people type, the names of devices, and the network each person connects from, shortened (the first three parts of an IPv4 address, the first three groups of an IPv6 one), never a whole IP address. The daily logs are deleted after 15 days; two running logs are cut back as they grow.
  • A copy of each usage summary (usage.jsonl), only if you agreed to usage statistics.
  • Your settings and presets, including which device plays into which input and the names you gave them.
  • Your licence file and live-link identity in ~/Library/Application Support/StreamDAW.

If you join a StreamDAW stream

This part is for listeners: anyone who opens a stream link or scans its QR code.

  • No account is needed. The name you type is seen by the host and by the other people in the session. If the host has turned on Snowstar sign-in and you are signed in, your account name is used instead. To find out, the page asks snowstar.company whether you are signed in.
  • Your connection. The page and its sound come from the host's Mac, through Cloudflare's network. For sound and video your browser connects directly to the host's Mac (WebRTC), so each side can see the other's IP address. To find that route, your browser asks a Google server (STUN) for its own public address. The host's Mac writes the name you type and a shortened form of your IP address in its logs (see "What stays on your Mac").
  • Microphone, camera and screen are used only after your browser asks you and you turn them on. They go to the host's Mac and are never stored by StreamDAW or sent to us. If the host records your microphone in their DAW, that recording belongs to the host: ask them if you want it removed. In sync mode on a phone, the microphone is used briefly to measure your phone's delay. That measurement happens in your browser, and only the resulting number is sent.
  • Your browser remembers your name, your mix, the devices you picked and a random device code, so the next visit picks up where you left off. Clearing the site's data in your browser removes it.
  • Connection quality (not your name and not your IP address) is sent to us by the host's app, as described above, unless the host switched stream health off.

Who else is involved

We do not sell, rent or trade personal data, and there are no advertising or analytics tools in StreamDAW. These services handle data for us:

  • Cloudflare (USA): runs our server, database and file storage, receives our email, and provides the tunnels that carry streams to listeners.
  • GitHub Pages (USA): serves the website's pages.
  • Resend (USA): sends our emails.
  • HYP (Israel): takes card payments and issues invoices.
  • Google (USA): supplies the typefaces on our pages and on StreamDAW's pages (it sees your IP address and browser when they load), helps browsers find a route to the host's Mac (STUN), and hosts the Gmail inbox where our own notifications reach us.
  • Google and Facebook: sign-in, only if you choose to sign in with them.

Data outside Israel

Some of these services store or process data outside Israel, mainly in the USA. For visitors from the EU: Israel is recognised by the European Commission as protecting personal data adequately, and our US providers Cloudflare and Resend are certified under the EU-US Data Privacy Framework.

Why we use it (for visitors from the EU and UK)

  • To provide what you asked for (a contract): your account, downloads, purchases, licence keys, the emails that deliver them, and replies to you.
  • Because the law requires it: order and payment records for tax.
  • Legitimate interests: keeping the service working and secure (stream health, which you can switch off; rate limits; a short request log), keeping live links working and stopping their abuse, binding a licence to one Mac, and fixing the problems people report.
  • Your consent: the newsletter, site analytics, and the app's usage statistics. You can withdraw it at any time. That does not affect what was done before.

How long we keep it

  • Your account and licence keys: until you ask us to delete them.
  • Orders and payment records: 7 years after the end of the tax year, because Israeli tax law requires it. This is true even if you ask us to delete everything else: we then strip them of your name, email and Mac, and delete them when the 7 years are up.
  • Copies of the emails we sent you: 2 years, or until your account is deleted.
  • Problem reports: 2 years.
  • Stream-health graphs: 14 days. Per-stream summaries: 90 days. Usage statistics: 24 months.
  • Live links: 180 days after they were last used.
  • Our request log: 14 days. Sign-in breadcrumbs: 30 days. Records used to slow down repeated sign-in attempts: one day.
  • On your Mac: the rewind buffer until the session ends; logs 15 days.

Your rights

You can ask us what we hold about you and for a copy of it, and ask us to correct it, delete it or stop using it. EU and UK residents can also ask us to restrict it, to hand it over in a portable form, and object to uses based on our legitimate interests. Write to hello@snowstar.company. We answer within 30 days.

When we delete a StreamDAW account, we keep only the order records the tax law requires, without your name or email. A deleted licence key cannot be sent again, but the app on your Mac keeps working with the key it already has.

If you think we have handled your data wrongly, you can complain to Israel's Privacy Protection Authority. In the EU or UK, you can complain to your local data protection authority.

No decision about you is made automatically.

Security

Passwords and sign-in sessions are stored only as one-way hashes, and so are download and password-reset links, except in the copy of the email that carried them (in your account's Messages; those links stop working after 7 days and 45 minutes). Licence keys are signed on our own computer, and the signing key never goes online. Everything travels over HTTPS, and card details never touch our systems.

Deleting your data

Email hello@snowstar.company and we will delete your account and everything attached to it — email address, name, favorites, sign-ins, StreamDAW key requests and keys, problem reports and the copies of the emails we sent you — within 30 days, usually the same day. What the law makes us keep stays: orders and payment records, stripped of your name and email, for 7 years after the end of the tax year, and music licences we granted, which are agreements with other people.

If you signed up through Facebook you can also use Settings → Apps and Websites → Snowstar → Remove. Facebook then notifies us automatically and the account is deleted on the spot.

Deletion is permanent. Anonymous, non-identifying counts (such as “this track was played 40 times”) remain, because they are not linked to any person.

Your rights

You can ask us what we hold about you, ask for it to be corrected, ask for a copy, or ask us to delete it. Write to hello@snowstar.company and a human will answer.
