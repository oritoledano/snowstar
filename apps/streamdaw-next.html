  1. Playback, with the whole team

    The producer goes on air from his studio. Five people open the link on their laptops: the creative director, the agency producer, the film director, the brand manager and the copywriter. Cameras on. He shares his screen and plays the score. They all hear his mix, live from the DAW, and talk over it as if they were sitting behind him.

  2. Someone at the door

    The voice-over artist opens the same link from her own studio. She waits in the lobby; nobody hears a thing until he lets her in.

  3. Her microphone, his track

    He makes a new track in Ableton with its input on StreamDAW Audio, and picks her name for that input. Her browser asks for a microphone. She chooses her own interface and her own mic, and reads the line. He brings her gain up. Her voice is on a track in his session.

  4. The brand manager solos her

    On her laptop the brand manager switches from Master mix to Track mixer, solos the voice-over and turns it up. She presses talkback: Warmer on the last line. The artist nods.

  5. Take two

    He records the new take and plays it back. Everyone hears the same thing at once, and everyone agrees: that’s the one.

Story two

The band

A live room at the start of a rehearsal. Eleven players, a set full of stems, and a phone in every pocket.

Photo to shoot A rehearsal room: a violinist holds her phone up to a QR code on a laptop screen, a wired earphone already in one ear, the drummer blurred behind her.

  1. The room

    Drums, bass, guitar, keys, a string quartet, two horns and percussion. In the corner a Mac runs the session: the stems go out through StreamDAW Audio, its headphone output feeds the PA, and a QR code is on the screen.

  2. One by one, a QR code

    Each player scans it with their own phone. No app to install, no account. Wired in-ears go in, a couple through a USB-C adapter. Play.

  3. A mix of their own

    The drummer wants the click loud and the strings low. The cellist wants the opposite. Every phone has its own mix (volume, pan, solo and mute on each stem) and remembers it for next time.

  4. Phone in the pocket

    Every phone plays the same instant, half a second behind the DAW, so nobody is ahead of anybody. The PA is held back by the same half second, so the room lands with the in-ears.

  5. “1, 2, 3, 4!”

    The guide track counts them in. Eleven people come in together, in time with the stems in their ears.

Before you start

Straight answers.

What do I need?

A Mac with Apple silicon, and a DAW that loads AU or VST3 plug-ins. StreamDAW installs one app, the plug-in and one audio device, StreamDAW Audio. Your listeners need only a browser and the link.

Which DAWs does it work with?

Logic Pro, Ableton Live, Cubase, Studio One, Reaper and Bitwig: anything that loads AU or VST3 on a Mac. Pro Tools (AAX) isn’t supported yet. Windows comes later.

What do listeners need?

A phone or a computer with a current browser, and the link or the QR code. Nothing to install, and no account unless you switch sign-in on. Headphones help.

How many people can join?

It’s made for a session’s worth of people: a band, a client team. Camera, screen share and talkback are for a handful of them at a time, and video is capped so it never hurts the audio.

Can we use Bluetooth earphones?

To listen, yes. To play in time with a band, use wired earphones (a USB-C adapter is fine). Bluetooth adds a delay of its own, different on every pair, that the phone can’t take out.

How good is the sound?

Lossless: 16-bit or 32-bit float at your project’s sample rate. On a thin connection a listener can switch to the Opus data saver. In-ear mode uses Opus, so that every phone can play the same instant.

Can I record someone who isn’t in the room?

Yes, with Pro. Pick a listener: their browser asks for a microphone and they choose their own interface or mic. Their voice arrives on one of StreamDAW Audio’s 16 inputs, with gain, and you record it on a DAW track. A remote take that lands exactly on the beat by itself is still being built, so for now expect to slide a take into place.

Is the picture in time with the music?

Not yet; we’re building it. Today camera and screen run beside the audio, and the audio always comes first.

How does the licence work?

Pro is one payment for one Mac: the key is made for that Mac’s machine ID. Changing Macs? Write to us and we’ll move it. Free never expires, and stops a session after 60 minutes. The 7-day trial gives you everything, once per Mac, with no card.
