Why your video sounds echoey.

The mic hears you once directly and then many more times off the walls. The further the mic is from your mouth, the more room it records.

  • EQ won't fix itReverb covers the same frequencies as your voice. Cutting them cuts you.
  • A noise gate won't fix itA gate mutes the gaps between words. The echo under the words stays.
  • Noise reduction won't fix itIt is built for steady sounds like a fan or hiss. A room's reflections keep changing.

What works is separating the voice from the room, which is what Studio Voice does.

Less echo next time.

From a studio that records voices for TV. None of it costs money.

  • Get the mic closer. A phone at arm's length records the room. The same phone a hand's width from your mouth records you.
  • Record facing something soft. A sofa, a bed, an open wardrobe full of clothes. Hard walls behind the camera are what you hear.
  • Pick the smaller, fuller room. A carpeted bedroom beats an empty living room.
  • Close the door and the window. Then listen to ten seconds on headphones before you record the rest.

Questions.

Can you remove echo from a video after recording?

Yes. Software that separates the voice from the room can take most of the reverb out after the fact. Audioctor does it on your Mac and exports the same video with the cleaned audio.

Is it free?

Does my video get uploaded?

No. Everything runs on your Mac, and Audioctor works offline.

Will removing echo make my voice sound robotic?

It can if you remove everything. Blend some of the original back in until it sounds like a real room that happens to be quiet.

Is echo the same as reverb?

People use both words for the same problem in video. Technically, echo is a distinct repeat and reverb is the wash of a room. Most echoey videos have reverb, and that is what Studio Voice removes.

Does it work on Intel Macs?

Yes, on macOS 14 or later. Intel Macs take longer than Apple silicon.
