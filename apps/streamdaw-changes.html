StreamDAW 0.7.0 20 October 2026

Added

  • Stream shows what you are doing as a room, with no title in the way. Sharing (the app on its own, Share a mix, a remote session, Compare's review): pick what to share, Spotify, Music, any app, everything this Mac plays or your DAW, and it goes on air at once; your listeners appear in one row in the order they came, each with their device. On stage: the stage plot, each musician on their spot with their instrument, in time or not, separate click and guide switches, the PA. Phones in Unison: the floor, how many phones play in time, the beat and the tempo from your DAW, the screens flashing on the beat, the QR full screen.
  • A mixer under every Stream view, closed until you open it: Main or anyone's own mix, pan, solo, mute and a fader per stem, Copy, Paste, Reset. Made bigger it shows every pair, each with its own ON (on air), its name (click it to rename) and its source menu: your DAW alone, another app, an input, everything this Mac plays, or StreamDAW's click.
  • One meter for what goes out: Stream's OUT, and a small ON AIR light with its level next to STOP LIVE in every other tab. The two big VU meters over every section are gone, so the middle of the window has that room back, and the pair 1/2 is called by what it carries (Spotify, This set…), never "Master".
  • Monitor is a pair of studio monitors and a faceplate: the output, what it plays, a volume knob, the delay, Line up with a lamp per phone. Sync mode's switch is there too, by the delay: With the phones or Direct. When sync stays on because someone is being recorded or your screen is shared, it says so right under it.
  • Analytics is a rack: everyone listening as a lamp, the person you pick with the last minute of their line, the loudness, what they hear, and how close each phone in sync is (± ms). While a camera or a screen is on, a folded Video panel under it shows what each viewer gets.
  • Compare is a tape deck: your versions on a shelf, who is hearing which one, a counter, the waveform with everyone's notes, and a light table for pictures.
  • Sessions is a cart machine for templates and the jobs (Broadcast's too), and a logbook for past sessions.
  • Settings is the back panel of the unit: inputs, the link, the talk button, your name, sharing, the licence.
  • Cameras and screens sit small at the bottom of the people column while anyone shares one; one click opens them over the whole middle, another puts them back.
  • Now playing while you share Spotify or Music: the track, its artist, its cover and where it is, with play/pause, next and previous right there; your listeners see the title and the artist on their page. macOS asks once per app, the first time you pick it or press one of its keys.
  • Your listeners' devices by name ("Galaxy S23", "iPhone 15 or 16", "iPad Air", "MacBook Pro 14-inch"), for you only and for this session only: never shown to the other listeners, never kept. Each phone in sync also tells you how close it plays to the room (± ms).
  • On stage keeps the click on 17/18 and the guide on 19/20 apart, both in the ears and never on the PA. The stage's tempo, time signature and beat come from Ableton Live's control surface, or from the plug-in in any DAW.
  • Phones in Unison can flash every phone's screen on the beat it plays (never more than three times a second), with a switch to turn it off.
  • The Monitor's volume knob turns down what you hear without touching a line-up's beeps, so lining up works at any volume.
  • Broadcast in the plug-in offers "This set" (your DAW's master) ready to pick, names your DAW, and draws your Mac as the model it is.
  • Zoom: ⌘ + and ⌘ − make StreamDAW's window bigger or smaller (67 % to 200 %), ⌘ 0 brings it back to 100 %, and a pinch on the trackpad does the same. The plug-in's window, the app's and a browser each remember their own, and your DAW no longer zooms its own window when you press them in StreamDAW's.
  • "No sound? Tap to fix" on the listener's page, under Play: one tap rebuilds the phone's sound (never a reload). When the phone notices by itself that nothing reaches its speaker and cannot bring it back, the button turns big and red.
  • Phones ask before they line up: in sync mode a phone first asks "Line up with the room?", only if you're in the same room as the speakers. Nothing beeps and no microphone is asked for until the person says Line up; "I'm not in the room" listens without beeps or microphone for the rest of the session.
  • Beeps say what they are: whenever a phone beeps (lining up, timing a take, tapping along) a card in the middle of its screen says what it is and how many seconds are left, with Stop.
  • "What are we doing today?": a moment after StreamDAW opens (its window or the plug-in's), pick what you came to do: broadcast what your Mac plays, share a mix, a remote session, perform with in-ears, compare versions, phones in Unison (every phone and speaker on one beat), or show me everything. Seven tiles, each with a small moving drawing of the job. StreamDAW sets itself up for that job with a template of its own, opens on the section it needs first, and puts three steps in a corner that tick themselves as you do them. Close it to keep the job you have; tick "Don't show this again" and it stops coming (it never comes while you're on air or someone is connected). Change the job any time in Sessions. In the free version, the Pro jobs say so and offer the 7-day trial.
  • Two phones on one singer, aligned by themselves: when two or more phones record the same sound in one take (only one of them in the singer's ears), StreamDAW lines the others up with that one, to the sample, by the sound itself, and puts them in Live already aligned. The Record row says so, for example "Input 2 aligned to Input 1 (+143 ms)". Phones recording different sounds are left where they are. In Live 11 the Record row says where to drag it.
  • Linked stereo inputs: link two Record inputs next to each other for one person, for a keyboard's left and right, while someone else sings on a mono input. Their browser on a computer with an interface sends both channels, untouched; the left lands on the first input and the right on the second, in time, with one gain and one timing for both. Their clean take is stereo too. A phone gives one channel: StreamDAW puts it on both inputs and says so on the Record row. A listener given their own track in Ableton Live can record the pair on a stereo track.
  • The People column, as wide as you want: drag its left edge to widen or narrow it (or the arrow keys on that edge; a double click puts it back). This computer remembers it, and the middle keeps its room in a small window.
  • The pictures over your DAW: while you work in your DAW, everyone's camera and shared screen sit small at the bottom of the screen your DAW is on, in a little window of their own that stays above the DAW without taking its keyboard. It comes by itself when a guest's camera or screen appears and StreamDAW's window with the pictures isn't open, and goes when the pictures stop. Drag it anywhere (it remembers where), click a picture to open StreamDAW on it, fold it to a small pill that says who is on camera, or hide it until the pictures change. In the plug-in's window, Mini closes that window and keeps the pictures at the bottom of the screen.
  • Your microphone and the music on speakers: while your DAW or an app plays out loud on your Mac's speakers (or a display's, or AirPlay), your microphone waits and opens the moment you talk over the music, so your guests no longer hear the music come back under their own voice. On headphones it stays as it is, and with hold to talk you decide. Under your microphone the control page says "Your mic waits while the music plays on speakers". A howl in your own microphone takes it out of the room for a few seconds, as a guest's does.
  • Your routing in Live, checked: when a track recording a remote performer plays straight out to a pair StreamDAW streams, StreamDAW tells you, and leaves that track out of the performer's own mix while they record. Your routing stays as you set it.
  • A tip for performers: in sync mode each phone says once: "Turn on Do Not Disturb (Focus) so calls don't cut your click." A web page cannot block calls, and the tip says so.
  • Compare (Pro), a new section after Record: link the mixes, cuts and pictures you want people to compare, straight from where they are on your Mac, with nothing copied. Number them by dragging, rename them for your listeners, and see each file's format, length, sample rate, bit depth and size. Moved files are followed; changed, missing and cloud-only files are shown as such.
  • Versions to compare for your listeners, under People: they switch between your audio versions while it plays, with no gap, and hear each file exactly as it is (an AIFF arrives as a WAV with the same samples). Press Play in Compare and everyone follows you, each on the version they chose; anyone can step off and come back with "Back to the host". Two listeners download at a time, and Compare never takes the upload your live stream needs.
  • Notes on a version: everyone sees each version's waveform, and can leave a note on a moment or on a stretch they drag across ("Add a note at 1:32"). Everyone in the session sees the notes with names. You can hide any note, and each person can delete their own. Notes stay with the list from session to session; "Copy as text" and "Save CSV…" take them away.
  • Video in Compare: link cuts, grades and edits like your mixes. Listeners watch them all at once in a grid, two to four side by side, or one big, and full screen, every picture in time with your Play. The sound is the version they choose, switched with no gap. H.264 plays as it is; for ProRes and anything else a browser can't play, StreamDAW makes an H.264 copy on your Mac and shows how far it has got. Anyone can draw a rectangle on a picture and leave a note on it.
  • Pictures in Compare: link covers, artwork and stills next to your mixes and cuts. Listeners see them all at once, side by side, one big or full screen, and leave notes on a part of a picture or on all of it. JPEG, PNG, GIF and WebP arrive exactly as they are; for HEIC, TIFF, PSD, RAW and anything larger than 4096 pixels, StreamDAW makes a copy on your Mac that keeps the colour profile. With file details on, listeners also see each picture's format, size, colour profile and bit depth.
  • Compare, from the app: "Add files" opens macOS's own file window, and StreamDAW remembers each file it links, so a file you move or rename, even into another folder, is found again. When you press Play in Compare, you hear it on your Monitor's output (whichever device the Monitor uses) at the same moment as your listeners, and it never goes into StreamDAW Audio.
  • Remove all in Compare, after a yes: your files stay where they are, and the list with all its notes is kept in Past sessions.
  • Sessions (where Presets was): Templates, and Past sessions. Each session is kept on your Mac by itself when it ends: when, how long, who came, the template, the Compare list and all its notes. Open one to read its notes, copy them or save them as CSV, put its list and notes back into Compare for a follow-up session, or delete it. Nothing of it leaves your Mac, and the chat is not kept.
  • Templates say what they are in a few words, with small icons for what each one switches on: the waiting room, sync mode, the click, your camera and microphone, a Record input and more.
  • Review tools for a listener you let control playback in Ableton Live: step back and forward by Live's own grid (the buttons show it, "−1/4" / "+1/4"), jump to the previous or next locator, tap a note or locator in their list to go there, and loop a part (a note's stretch, or one, two or four bars either side). Your own loop comes back exactly as it was when they stop, leave or lose control. Tempo, tracks and your edits stay out of their reach.
  • Clean takes: while you record a phone into Ableton Live, the phone also keeps the take itself, exactly as its microphone gave it, nothing lost. After the take it sends it to your Mac (slowly while you are on air, so your live stream never pays for it) and carries on where it stopped if the connection drops. In Live 12, StreamDAW puts it on the track exactly where the live take landed, in place of it, as one undo step. In Live 11, the Record row tells you which file to drag in and where. The Record row shows how far each clean take has arrived and how many gaps the live take had.
  • At its first start StreamDAW connects your Mac to your Snowstar account, so your trial, licence and Macs are in one place. Settings › Licence shows it, and offers it until it is done.
  • StreamDAW puts its control surface into Ableton Live by itself. If Live is not connected, the control page shows the two steps to switch it on.
  • A Stop button on every line-up: the beeps end, the microphone closes and the last good timing stays.
  • Mic level on the phone while you are being recorded (−24 to +12 dB). The host's gain on a recording input now goes down too (−24 to +36 dB), for a microphone that arrives too hot.
  • StreamDAW says what to do when it is not in your DAW's list of plug-ins.
  • Before the first take, a phone measures its own delay on the microphone it records with, once per speaker and microphone: three soft beeps in about 2 seconds on the loudspeaker (the phone says so while they play), 8 taps on earphones. It remembers it for 12 hours. Your Record row shows which was used: measured now, kept from before, or tapped.
  • A phone far from your room stops trying to line up with it after one try ("Room out of earshot").
  • Record MIDI from a keyboard player anywhere: give them a MIDI track in Ableton Live. They play their own keyboard in Chrome, hear it at once, and each take lands in place as one clip.
  • While your DAW records someone on a Record input, the Monitor plays the take as it will land: your playback and their voice together, one second later, so you can judge the take while it happens. It goes back to normal the moment recording stops.
  • Chat notifications: when someone writes and the chat is closed, the edge of the window glows lime for a moment and a slim bar at the top says who and what, wherever you have scrolled (several messages stack as "Name and 2 more"). Tap it to open the chat at that message. A listener also hears a short, soft chime when nothing is playing on their page; there is never a sound while music or Compare plays, while their microphone is open or during a take, and never on your own control page.
  • Record, as a recording booth: a row of lit pads, one per input, shows at a glance who is on each one (lime: sound coming in, amber: waiting, dark: off), and a linked stereo pair is one wide pad. Click a pad and the booth below shows that input: an empty mic waiting with "Who records here?" (a person, the first guest to join, everyone, or a track), or the person with their sound on its way, how late their phone hears itself and where the take lands in your DAW. Underneath, a hardware faceplate: the input's level meter, a gain knob (drag it, scroll, use the arrow keys, double-click for 0 dB), the timing nudge, the clean take's lamp and the stereo switch. StreamDAW remembers the input you last looked at.
  • Analytics for listeners in the look of your own: their connection (delay, buffer, jitter, loss, dropouts, bit rate, in sync mode how close in time they play), the loudness of what they hear in LUFS on your meter, and a live spectrum of what they hear on the same dark screen. All measured on their own device, twice a second, only while they have it open; folded, it costs their phone nothing, and it never adds work for the relay.
  • MIDI in Record: everyone you give one of Ableton Live's MIDI tracks gets a pad of their own after the inputs, marked MIDI, with their name and the track. Its booth shows their keyboard and which one they play, the notes arriving as a small piano roll, and your MIDI track, armed or recording. + MIDI gives someone a MIDI track right there: pick the person and the track. It shows while Live is connected and your set has a MIDI track.
  • Turn an input off from its pad: click the pad that is already selected (or press Enter or Space on it) and that input goes off, with "Input 3 off · Undo" for a few seconds. A linked stereo pair goes off as one. On a MIDI pad, the same click takes the track back.
  • A guest's take starts with their microphone: when a guest is already talking to you, or turns their microphone on, the take's microphone opens with it, with no second tap on their side. Until it does, Record says why nothing arrives: "Waiting for them to tap Mic on", or that their microphone is blocked, missing or silent.
  • A take's level that looks after itself: the first second of a guest's microphone sets their phone's Mic level so the peaks land well below clipping (it never turns a quiet microphone up). If the take clips anyway, it turns down at once; if the phone's own microphone overloads, the guest is asked to hold it further away. Record and a short note tell you each time. Your gain stays yours.
  • A voice arriving too late, said: when a guest far away sends their voice later than a take allows, Record says "Arriving too late" with how late, as it happens, and that the clean take will replace the take.
  • Every section is always in the left column, whatever the job: no More / Less.
  • A clean take you can see coming, keep out, or undo: while a phone's clean take is on its way to your Mac, Record shows it on that input's pad ("CLEAN 45%") and on its faceplate, with the time it has left ("Arriving · 45% · ~20 s"). Until it lands, Keep the live take leaves your take as it was recorded (the clean file stays in the takes folder). Once it is in Live, Undo clean take puts the live take back where it was, in one click.

Fixed

  • An iPhone could play from its earpiece (quietly, slightly out of time) after lining itself up, and only a reload brought the speaker back. A phone now closes its microphone the moment a line-up is done, and keeps it only while you have given that person a track or a Record input. If the sound still lands on the earpiece the phone puts it back on the speaker by itself, and the page's output menu on an iPhone offers one thing: "Sound from the earpiece? Tap to fix" (iOS doesn't let a page pick the speaker).
  • Record's "Your DAW" could name the wrong track for a person being recorded: the first of the many tracks on that input in Ableton Live (often a drum track). It now names the track that really records them: the one you gave them, else the one armed on that input, with its arm, Monitor and recording as Live has them. With no track armed it says "Arm a track on IN 1 · Monitor Off" without guessing a name, and it says when Live is not connected.
  • A guest's phone started beeping as soon as it joined a session that records it, and nobody was told why. Before those three soft beeps (they measure the phone's delay so takes land on the beat) the phone now asks: "The studio will record you on Input 1 · First your phone measures its delay: 3 soft beeps", with OK, measure and Not now. You see it on Record's pad and scene and in a message; when sync is on because of that input, Record and the Stream section say so. Not now leaves their takes untimed until they tap Measure now, and Record says that too.
  • Words you can read: no words you need to read in StreamDAW's window are smaller than 12 points any more, the new views included (13 for a sentence), Record's booth included: the timing, your DAW's track, the clean take, the stereo switch. Long explanations are shorter on screen, with the whole sentence when you point at them, and nothing is cut off.
  • A singer recorded from their phone heard the music half a second late, so their voice reached your DAW at the very edge of the time a take allows, and takes came in with gaps. While a phone's take microphone is open it now plays the music as soon as it reaches it, about a quarter of a second sooner, so the voice arrives with room to spare. Everyone else hears as before.
  • On some iPhones the music a singer heard wobbled in pitch and jumped about once a minute, and their takes drifted against the music, so a clean take could land out of time. StreamDAW now follows a phone's audio clock however far off it runs, a singer's playback keeps one steady speed through a take, and the take follows what the phone really recorded.
  • While someone sings into a take, their phone's own camera and screen no longer take the upload their voice needs.
  • On an iPhone, after a pause (the phone locked, earphones plugged in, a call), the page could show the music moving in its meters while playing nothing, and only a reload brought the sound back. The phone now notices within seconds and restarts its sound by itself, its microphone too when that is what holds the sound; the meters show silence while nothing plays.
  • A phone asked for the microphone a second time a minute or two after it had been allowed. It now keeps the microphone it was allowed, switched off while nothing uses it, for up to two minutes after its last use, so what follows (lining up with the room, Arm again, the next take) asks nothing again; meanwhile an iPhone plays as it does with no microphone. It lets it go when you turn your microphone off (Mic, the take's switch, Disarm), after those two minutes, or when you leave.
  • Wired EarPods are recognised as earphones.
  • With StreamDAW open on its own, no DAW, an app you had put on 1/2 (Spotify, Music, a browser) never went out: StreamDAW waited for the master from its plug-in instead, so a phone pressing Play heard nothing and the meters stayed still. An app, an input or the click on a pair you send now goes out by itself, with or without a DAW. When your DAW also plays through the StreamDAW plug-in on its master bus, the app goes out while it plays and your DAW's master while the app is quiet (one or the other), and StreamDAW says so.
  • With your DAW sending stems into StreamDAW Audio, only the pairs switched on in StreamDAW went out: a template (the one "Share a mix" sets up has the master alone) or your last session could leave your DAW's stems off, so listeners saw none of them in their track mixer, and a track you moved to another pair went silent. Every pair your DAW plays into now goes out by itself, named as in your DAW (in Ableton Live, as soon as a track's output is set to it; in other DAWs when it plays), and in Live a pair switched on that way goes off again when no track plays into it any more; 1/2, the full mix, stays as your template or you set it. Switch a pair off with its ON and it stays off. An app you put on a pair with StreamDAW open on its own gives that pair back when your DAW plays into it, and StreamDAW says so.
  • On an iPhone, when the page's sound stopped and a new start of it went unanswered, the page waited silently, without "No sound? Tap to fix", until it was reloaded. It now goes on after a moment and shows the button; one tap brings the sound back.
  • Switching StreamDAW off in your DAW (in Ableton Live, the device's on/off button) let the stream go on for ten seconds and then ended the session for everyone. Now it stops the stream at once: you are off air, as with STOP LIVE, and everyone stays in the session. Switch it on again and you are back on air, if you were on air when you switched it off. While it is off, the control page says "StreamDAW is switched off in your DAW" and GO LIVE waits for it. When your DAW's own audio stops (changing its audio settings, the Mac going to sleep), nothing changes. Removing StreamDAW from your DAW, closing the project or quitting the DAW still ends the session after ten seconds.
  • A guest's phone on its own speaker, recorded on two linked Record inputs, recorded without its echo protection and sent twice the audio it had; from far away nothing of it reached your DAW in time, and the right input was silent. A phone on linked inputs now records one channel, protected on its speaker, the same on both inputs.
  • From far away, a guest's take could miss most of its audio without a word. The phone now drops what can no longer arrive in time instead of queuing it, so the take keeps up; Record says how late the voice arrives; the clean take fills the gaps after the take.
  • The clean take did not go into Ableton Live when the set had a group track: Live refused it and the broken live take stayed. It now goes onto the track that recorded the take; if Live has no such track, Record says why and where to drag the file.
  • After a guest reloaded their page, their takes could be placed with no timing at all and land late. The phone now uses the timing it measured on that speaker before.
  • A guest heard their own voice come back a moment later when your microphone was open next to your speakers. Your microphone now waits while a guest's voice plays out loud, as it does for the music, and opens when you talk over it.
  • A guest on a weak connection saw your camera and screen switch off and on every half minute. Their video now holds a lower setting while the connection keeps stumbling, instead of flickering.
  • A take's microphone that went silent after the guest switched microphones now opens again by itself.
  • Record's Stereo switch no longer changes with the Space bar (your DAW's play key) or a double click, and says what it did, with Undo.
  • A clean take recorded from the middle of a bar landed early in Ableton Live: in one take, nearly a second early. It now lands exactly where Live recorded the live take, to the sample. If its sound and its timing ever disagree by more than a tenth of a second, StreamDAW places it by its timing and says why on the Record row, instead of moving it.
  • A clean take could be matched against an earlier take's clean take on the same track. It now meets only the take Live just recorded.
  • A phone being recorded beeped again every time its page loaded. It now measures its delay once per speaker and microphone and remembers it for 12 hours.
  • When you turned off a guest's camera, microphone or screen, they could not turn it back on, even by reloading their page. Now your click turns it off and they can turn it on again with one tap. Click again to keep it off; the same switch gives it back. Stop all also gives back whatever you kept off.
  • Under the first four Record inputs, a column of link buttons showed for the inputs folded away. They fold with their inputs now.
  • Recording a guest, you heard them twice: live in the conversation, and again a second later in your Monitor's take. While a take records, the people you hear in the take are no longer heard live as well, and the conversation comes back the moment the take ends. Listeners who hear the take ("· live") are spared the double too. The performer still hears you.
  • With Live's record File Type set to AIFF or FLAC, the clean take could not replace Live's live take ("Live's live take could not be read (not a WAV file)"). WAV, AIFF and FLAC all work now.
  • On an iPhone, a phone call during a performance could leave the page silent after the call. It now takes its sound back by itself and plays in time with everyone again.
  • A clean take could wait forever to upload while you were on air, if the phone had thought you were off air. It now goes at the on-air pace.
  • StreamDAW's own window showed only the names of the people on camera while the video ran in the plug-in's window inside your DAW. It now shows their pictures too.
  • In a small window, a person's buttons pushed their name out of the People column. Now the name comes first, on two lines if it needs them, beside one button for what they may do: a click lets them control playback (green ▶), the next lets them record into a track you pick from the menu that opens (red ring), the next takes both back. Their microphone, camera, screen and Dismiss are under ⋯.
  • On the listener's page, playback control and recording were two cards, each with its own Play. They are one card now: the transport and the review tools, and, when you give them a track, the record buttons under the same transport. A MIDI track works the same way.
  • Takes recorded live from a phone had tiny clicks: a sample came out twice, many times a second. Every sample now arrives once.
  • The app's icon no longer leaves the Dock when you close its window. Click the icon to bring the window back.
  • A guest's camera could be pushed out of view by your own camera and shared screens. Guests now come first.
  • Recordings from an iPhone on its loudspeaker landed about 80 ms late.
  • A phone recorded into Live on a track with Monitor on Auto landed late. StreamDAW now keeps that track on Monitor Off.
  • On a Mac in Low Power Mode, a guest's camera could appear as a black picture with a Play button. Cameras and screens now play by themselves; if macOS still holds one back, the picture says so and plays at your next click anywhere.
  • Sync mode no longer tries to line up the room when your speakers play straight from your DAW, or when the Monitor has no output: no beeps play, and the control page says, under the sync switch, what to set. Phones on earphones still play in sync.
  • A line-up whose first round measures cleanly ends there ("Lined up in one round"), instead of beeping a second round to check.
  • A phone that comes back within 12 hours (a reload, a reconnect, a new session) keeps its line-up when its audio output and your Monitor are the same: nothing beeps when it presses Play. A different output or Monitor lines it up again.
  • A phone that came back from its lock screen could stay silent while it showed it was playing (an iPhone, after its microphone opened for a recording). It now notices within two seconds and brings the sound back by itself, in time, with no reload.
  • In Record, a guest on a computer was drawn with a small keyboard beside their laptop, which looked like MIDI, and a MIDI track you gave someone did not show in Record at all. A computer is now drawn with a microphone, and a keyboard always means MIDI.
  • On a guest's page, the MIDI keyboard picker had a large empty space above and below it and no label. It now sits on one line labelled "Input", like the microphone picker, and lists every keyboard to choose from.
