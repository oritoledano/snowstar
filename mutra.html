Before you license

Questions worth asking

What am I actually buying?

A licence to use one track in one project, for a set length of time. You are not buying the music itself — Snowstar keeps the master and the publishing — but for the term of your licence the right to use it in your video is yours, in writing, with a certificate naming you, the track and the project.

How is the price worked out?

Three questions, in this order: who is buying, where it runs, and for how long. A filmmaker cutting their own reel and an insurer running a brand campaign are not the same buyer and do not pay the same fee for the same track. Pick your answers on the track page and the price appears before you commit to anything — no quote form, no waiting, no per-second maths.

Is it royalty-free?

You pay one fee and nothing else — we never invoice you again for the same use, and there is no per-play or per-view charge coming later. What we do not do is pretend the composer's performing-rights share vanishes: if your film is broadcast, the broadcaster's blanket PRO payment still reaches the writer, exactly as it does for every other piece of music on air. That costs you nothing and changes nothing about your licence.

Can I use it in a TV commercial or in cinema?

Yes, but not through the card checkout. Web, social, podcast, internal and industrial video are Standard coverage and you can buy them yourself in a couple of minutes. TV, cinema, VOD and OTT, festivals, radio and commercials are Extended coverage — the terms are negotiated per campaign, so those go through us. Ask, and you get a number back the same day.

How long does a licence last, and what happens at the end?

Six, twelve, twenty-four or thirty-six months — longer terms cost less per month. There is also a no-end-date option, limited to organic distribution, because a licence with no expiry and no ceiling on paid media is an unlimited ad buy sold once at a website price.

When a term ends, the video does not have to come down: renew and it simply continues. We write to you before the date, not after it.

One licence per video, or per track?

Per track, per project. One track in one campaign is one licence, and it covers every cut and every length of that campaign — the 30, the 15, the 6, the vertical. A second campaign is a second licence, even if it is the same track.

Is the music really cleared? Who wrote it?

It was written in-house at Snowstar, most of it originally scored to picture for Israeli and international campaigns. That is the whole point of this catalogue: one owner, one signature, no sample-clearance chain, no label to chase, nobody who can appear a year later with a claim. Where a track has co-writers, they are declared on the track and the fee splits on our ledger exactly as declared.

I need an invoice for accounting.

Every purchase produces a proper Israeli tax invoice (חשבונית מס) issued to the company details you give at checkout, emailed with the licence certificate. Prices shown are before VAT. Buying from outside Israel? Say so and we will issue accordingly.

I have a brief and no time to listen to 300 tracks.

Then don't. Use I have a brief at the top of the page: describe the film in a sentence — the mood, the length, what happens on screen — and the catalogue reorders itself around it. If the shortlist still isn't right, write to us and a human sends back five tracks with reasons.

Can you write something original instead?

That is Snowstar's day job and has been for over twenty years — scoring to picture, arrangement, recording, mix. The catalogue is what that work leaves behind. If nothing here fits, tell us about the film.

What if I buy the wrong licence?

Write to us before you publish and we will fix it — upgrade the coverage, change the term, swap the track. The full terms are in the licence agreement and the refund policy, both in plain language and both short.

Still unsure? Ask us directly — questions about licensing are answered by the person who wrote the music.
